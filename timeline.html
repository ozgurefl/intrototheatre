ENG214 Introduction to Theatre

Key Ideas

  • Origins of Western drama
  • Religious festivals honoring Dionysus
  • Development of tragedy and comedy
  • Large outdoor amphitheatres
  • Use of masks and chorus

Important Playwrights

  • Aeschylus
  • Sophocles
  • Euripides
  • Aristophanes
  • Plautus
  • Terence
Remember: Greek theatre emphasized fate and the gods, while Roman theatre focused more on entertainment and spectacle.
Knowledge Check:
What group commented on the action of a Greek play?

The Chorus.

Medieval Theatre

  • Mystery Plays
  • Miracle Plays
  • Morality Plays
  • Performed by guilds

Renaissance Theatre

  • Renewed interest in classical learning
  • Permanent theatres developed
  • Humanism influenced drama

Major Figure

  • William Shakespeare
Remember: Renaissance theatre shifted attention from religious themes to human experience.
Knowledge Check:
What cultural movement influenced Renaissance drama?

Humanism.

Key Features

  • Theatres reopened after Puritan rule
  • Women appeared on stage professionally
  • Comedy of Manners became popular
  • Focus on wit and social satire
Remember: Restoration comedy often mocked upper-class behaviour.
Knowledge Check:
What major change occurred regarding performers?

Women were allowed to perform on stage.

Key Characteristics

  • Emotion over reason
  • Heroic individuals
  • Spectacular staging
  • Clear heroes and villains
Remember: Melodrama aimed to create strong emotional reactions from audiences.
Knowledge Check:
What is the defining feature of melodrama?

Strong emotional storytelling with clear moral oppositions.

Key Features

  • Everyday life portrayed accurately
  • Psychological depth
  • Detailed realistic settings
  • Social issues explored

Important Playwrights

  • Henrik Ibsen
  • Anton Chekhov
  • Émile Zola
Remember: Realism asks "How do ordinary people actually live?"
Knowledge Check:
Which playwright wrote A Doll's House?

Henrik Ibsen.

Modernist Ideas

  • Experimentation
  • Breaking traditional structures
  • Symbolism and expressionism

Theatre of the Absurd

  • Meaninglessness of existence
  • Circular dialogue
  • Illogical situations

Key Playwrights

  • Samuel Beckett
  • Eugène Ionesco
Knowledge Check:
What play is considered a landmark of Absurdism?

Waiting for Godot.

Key Trends

  • Political activism in theatre
  • Social criticism
  • Epic Theatre
  • Rise of Broadway musicals

Key Figures

  • Bertolt Brecht
  • Arthur Miller
  • Tennessee Williams
Remember: Brecht wanted audiences to think critically instead of becoming emotionally absorbed.
Knowledge Check:
What was Brecht's famous audience-distancing technique called?

The Alienation Effect (Verfremdungseffekt).

Current Trends

  • Diverse voices and perspectives
  • Global storytelling
  • Digital theatre
  • Immersive experiences
  • Identity and social justice themes
Remember: Contemporary theatre reflects an increasingly interconnected world.
Knowledge Check:
Name one way technology has influenced contemporary theatre.

Digital projections, livestream performances, multimedia staging, or virtual productions.
